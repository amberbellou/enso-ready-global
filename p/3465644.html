Main risks: extreme heat.

That season starts in about 3 months (Dec–Mar 2026-27).

During El Niño, Southeastern Brazil (São Paulo, Rio, Minas Gerais) usually sees rain close to normal and warmer than usual.

Sits between the wet south and the dry north; rain signal is mixed, heat is more likely. [iri_impacts]

There is no reliable local rain record for this season, so treat this as uncertain.

In the last 6 strong El Niño events, Dec–Mar rain here averaged 5% below normal. It was wetter than normal in 50% of them.

Most recent events: 2009-10: 12% below normal; 2015-16: 5% above normal; 2023-24: 30% below normal. [CHIRPS v2.0 monthly (UCSB Climate Hazards Center), 2° box average via IRI Data Library]

Global status: El Niño Advisory (issued 13 August 2026). [NOAA CPC]

What to do now

  1. 🌡️ Move hard work to early morning and late afternoon. (about 5 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Heat illness peaks in the middle of the day.

  2. 👵 Check daily on elderly neighbours and people living alone. (about 10 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Most heat deaths happen quietly at home.

  3. 💧 Drink water often, before you feel thirsty. (about 1 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Thirst comes late when the body is already short of water.

  4. 📢 Follow your national weather service for warnings; this briefing is a seasonal outlook, not a warning. (about 5 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Only official services issue alerts for specific days and places.

  5. 🗣️ Tell one neighbour what this season may bring. (about 5 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Communities that talk about risk early prepare better.
