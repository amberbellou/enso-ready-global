← Change place · Region: Coastal Peru and Ecuador

Quevedo: much more rain than usual is likely in Dec–Apr 2026-27 (the coastal rainy season).

Main risks: flooding, landslides and disease outbreaks.

That season starts in about 3 months (Dec–Apr 2026-27).

During El Niño, Coastal Peru and Ecuador usually sees much more rain than usual and warmer than usual.

Warm coastal water brings heavy rain to a normally dry coast. 1982-83, 1997-98 and 2017 brought severe floods. [cpc_impacts]

The local rain record agrees with the known pattern, so this outlook is likely.

In the last 6 strong El Niño events, Dec–Apr rain here averaged 26% above normal. It was wetter than normal in 50% of them.

Most recent events: 2009-10: 17% below normal; 2015-16: 9% below normal; 2023-24: 44% above normal. [CHIRPS v2.0 monthly (UCSB Climate Hazards Center), 2° box average via IRI Data Library]

Global status: El Niño Advisory (issued 13 August 2026). [NOAA CPC]

What to do now

  1. 📄 Put ID cards, land papers and phone numbers in a sealed plastic bag. (about 15 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Papers are the hardest thing to replace after a flood.

  2. 🚶 Agree with your household on a high place to go and a way to get there. (about 20 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Deciding in the dark, in the rain, is when people get hurt.

  3. 💧 Store clean drinking water in closed containers, enough for three days. (about 30 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Floods pollute wells and taps first.

  4. 🧹 Clear drains, gutters and channels near your home now. (about 60 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Blocked drains turn heavy rain into a flood in your yard.

  5. 📦 Move food, seeds, fertiliser and valuables off the floor. (about 45 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Even shallow water ruins stored food and seed.

  6. 📻 Find out how your local warnings are given: radio, SMS, loudspeaker or a neighbour. (about 10 minutes)
    Why this matters

    The official warning is the signal to act.

  7. 💊 Keep a week of any daily medicines in the emergency bag. (about 10 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Clinics may be cut off for days.

  8. 👀 Watch for new cracks, tilting trees or springs on slopes above your home. (about 10 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Slopes give warning signs before they fail.

  9. 🏠 If you live below a steep slope, arrange a safer place to sleep during heavy rain. (about 20 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Landslides mostly strike at night after days of rain.

  10. 🧪 Boil or treat drinking water when floods reach wells or pipes. (about 10 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Cholera and diarrhoea spread through flood water.
