El Progreso: less rain than usual is uncertain in May–Oct 2026 (the rainy season and canícula).

Main risks: drought, crop failure and food shortages.

That season is happening now (May–Oct 2026).

During El Niño, Central America and southern Mexico usually sees less rain than usual and warmer than usual.

Rainy season starts late and the mid-summer dry spell (canícula) is longer, especially in the Dry Corridor. [cpc_impacts]

Past events here were mixed, so treat this as uncertain.

In the last 6 strong El Niño events, May–Oct rain here averaged 5% above normal. It was wetter than normal in 50% of them.

Most recent events: 2009-10: 57% above normal; 2015-16: 25% below normal; 2023-24: 6% below normal. [CHIRPS v2.0 monthly (UCSB Climate Hazards Center), 2° box average via IRI Data Library]

Global status: El Niño Advisory (issued 13 August 2026). [NOAA CPC]

What to do now

  1. 💧 Fix leaks and add water storage: tanks, drums, or a covered pit. (about 120 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Every litre saved now is a litre you do not have to carry later.

  2. 🍚 Buy staple food a little at a time now while prices are normal. (about 30 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Food prices rise fastest when harvests fail.

  3. 👥 Agree with neighbours how a shared well or tank will be rationed. (about 45 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Agreed rules prevent conflict when water gets short.

  4. 💧 Store water and plan irrigation if you are in the north or east. (about 60 minutes)
    Why this matters

    El Niño summers are often dry there.

  5. 🤝 Find out which local groups give food or cash support and how to register. (about 30 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Registering early avoids queues when help arrives.

  6. 📢 Follow your national weather service for warnings; this briefing is a seasonal outlook, not a warning. (about 5 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Only official services issue alerts for specific days and places.

  7. 🗣️ Tell one neighbour what this season may bring. (about 5 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Communities that talk about risk early prepare better.
