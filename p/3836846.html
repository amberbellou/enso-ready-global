San Nicolás de los Arroyos: more rain than usual is likely in Oct–Mar 2026-27 (spring and summer).

Main risks: flooding and river flooding.

That season starts next month (Oct–Mar 2026-27).

During El Niño, Southeastern South America usually sees more rain than usual and temperatures close to normal.

Southern Brazil, Uruguay, northeastern Argentina and Paraguay usually get heavy spring and summer rain and river floods. [cpc_impacts]

Past events here were mixed, so treat this as likely.

In the last 6 strong El Niño events, Oct–Mar rain here averaged close to normal. It was wetter than normal in 50% of them.

Most recent events: 2009-10: 50% above normal; 2015-16: 14% below normal; 2023-24: close to normal. [CHIRPS v2.0 monthly (UCSB Climate Hazards Center), 2° box average via IRI Data Library]

Global status: El Niño Advisory (issued 13 August 2026). [NOAA CPC]

What to do now

  1. 📄 Put ID cards, land papers and phone numbers in a sealed plastic bag. (about 15 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Papers are the hardest thing to replace after a flood.

  2. 🚶 Agree with your household on a high place to go and a way to get there. (about 20 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Deciding in the dark, in the rain, is when people get hurt.

  3. 💧 Store clean drinking water in closed containers, enough for three days. (about 30 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Floods pollute wells and taps first.

  4. 🧹 Clear drains, gutters and channels near your home now. (about 60 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Blocked drains turn heavy rain into a flood in your yard.

  5. 📦 Move food, seeds, fertiliser and valuables off the floor. (about 45 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Even shallow water ruins stored food and seed.

  6. 📻 Find out how your local warnings are given: radio, SMS, loudspeaker or a neighbour. (about 10 minutes)
    Why this matters

    The official warning is the signal to act.

  7. 💊 Keep a week of any daily medicines in the emergency bag. (about 10 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Clinics may be cut off for days.

  8. 🌊 Learn where the river reached in the last big flood and stay above that line. (about 20 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Past high-water marks are the best local guide.

  9. 📢 Follow your national weather service for warnings; this briefing is a seasonal outlook, not a warning. (about 5 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Only official services issue alerts for specific days and places.

  10. 🗣️ Tell one neighbour what this season may bring. (about 5 minutes)
    Why this matters

    Communities that talk about risk early prepare better.
