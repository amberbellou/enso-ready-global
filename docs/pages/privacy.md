# Privacy

ENSO Ready does not collect personal data. This page says what stays on your device, which other services your browser contacts, and how to clear everything.

## What we do not collect

- No accounts. There is nothing to sign up for.
- No cookies.
- No analytics or usage statistics.
- No tracking pixels, fingerprinting or advertising.
- No log of the places people search for or the briefings they open.
- We never sell or share data, because we never have any.

The site is a set of static files. There is no server of ours that receives anything from you.

## What stays on your device

The app remembers a few things in your browser's local storage so you do not have to enter them again. They never leave your device.

- Your chosen place (name and coordinates).
- What you do for a living, if you chose an option.
- Your language.
- Your display settings, such as text size and reduced motion.
- Your last briefing, so it can be shown offline.
- Which preparation steps you have ticked.

If you tap "Use my location", the browser asks your permission first. The coordinates are used to find your grid cell and are stored like a chosen place. You can pick a place by typing or on the map instead.

## Which other services your browser contacts, and why

The app only makes network calls when it needs something to work. Each one goes to a service with its own privacy policy. Like any web server, each one can see your IP address.

**GitHub Pages (the host).** Every page and data file comes from here. GitHub may keep standard server logs. We cannot see them. See [GitHub's privacy statement](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement).

**The place index, also on GitHub Pages.** When you type a place name, the app fetches a small index file whose name is the first letters of what you typed. So the host sees those letters, not your full search or your final choice.

**Open-Meteo.** When you open a briefing, your browser asks Open-Meteo for this season's ECMWF forecast at your place. Open-Meteo receives the latitude and longitude of that place. It does not receive your name, settings or anything else. If the request fails, the briefing still works. Forecast data is CC BY 4.0. See [Open-Meteo's terms and privacy page](URL to be confirmed).

**OpenStreetMap tiles, only if you tap the map.** The map is off until you open it. When you do, your browser fetches map images from OpenStreetMap for the area you look at. See the [OpenStreetMap Foundation privacy policy](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Privacy_Policy). Opening the map also loads the Leaflet map library from cdnjs.cloudflare.com, a code hosting service.

That is the complete list. No fonts, scripts or images are loaded from anywhere else.

## Machine-translated pages

Some languages are machine-translated and show a banner. Tapping the banner opens a GitHub issue page. GitHub's own rules apply there. You choose what to write, and you do not need to include personal details.

## How to clear your data

Everything is in your browser, so you clear it in your browser. Open your browser's settings, find "site data" or "cookies and site data", and delete the entry for this site. This removes your place, settings, ticked steps and saved briefing. The app then starts fresh.

You can also use a private or incognito window. Nothing is kept after you close it.

## Children

The site asks for no personal details from anyone, of any age.

## Questions

ENSO Ready is built and run by one person. For questions about privacy, [open an issue on GitHub](https://github.com/amberbellou/enso-ready-global/issues). You can also email amberbellou@gse.harvard.edu.

If you write to that address, your message and your email address reach a personal mailbox, the way any email does. It is used to answer you and nothing else: not added to a list, not shared, not used to identify you on the site. The site itself still receives nothing from you. If you would rather not use email, a GitHub issue works and does not need your name.
